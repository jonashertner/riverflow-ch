A filled disc is a gauge with a temperature series, and it is filled from the ramp. A hollow ring is a gauge with none: an absent reading is not a cold one. The rivers keep their discharge colour under this layer, dimmed, because temperature is measured at the gauge and nowhere else.

One reading is one sensor. Open a gauge to see it against the others nearby before you rely on it.

Two registers, and they do not describe the same objects. The dams are structures, with the volume each reservoir holds when full. The filling level is a weekly figure in GWh — stored electricity, not stored water — and it is published for four regions, not for 225 dams. So this layer can say what a reservoir holds when full, and it can say how full the region is this week. It cannot say how full the Grande Dixence is today. Nobody publishes that.

The 2023 bodies are drawn over the 1850 outlines. What stays in colour is the ice that has gone. Press play to run six dated surveys in their real intervals.

 

This is area, not volume. The 2010 and 2016 totals use different delineation methods; their apparent increase is not ice growth. Through recent decades the ice thinned faster than it shrank in plan, because a glacier loses thickness over its whole surface before it loses its outline. The area figures here are measured. The volume figures are cited, and they come from separate work by separate methods.

Takes water out

Puts water in

Two colours, because the direction is what the law turns on: Art. 31 GSchG governs taking water out, GSchV Annex 2 No. 12(4) governs putting heat in. Which register a mark belongs to is carried by the shape of the mark, not by a third and fourth colour.

A filled disc carries a quantity. An open ring carries a place and nothing more. Of the four federal registers only the hydropower statistic yields a discharge, and it yields it by arithmetic, not by measurement: capacity divided by head. The residual-flow map holds no volume at all, and no cooling volume for the nuclear sites is published as open data. Treatment plants carry a ratio instead: their discharge against the receiving water at Q347. For 39 of them it is above 100 %, which is to say the brook at low flow carries less than the plant puts into it.

 

     

    The water register is protected by ordinance, the statutory one by the Constitution. Alluvial zones and amphibian spawning sites state their aim as water, so they are drawn in the water's hue. Bogs, fens and mire landscapes are held by Art. 78(5) BV, the Rothenthurm article, which forbids building and any alteration of the ground save for the mires’ own protection and their existing agricultural use — with no balancing test to lose, and so they wear the ink every statutory quantity here wears. How this is drawn.

    The ages disagree with the aims. The amphibian inventory was revised months ago; the alluvial-zone inventory, whose aim is a quantity of water, carries a federal data state of November 2017 — and so does the inventory holding Rothenthurm. Every date.

    Three of these four are pictures, not data. The groundwater bodies, the protection zones and the catchments are drawn by a federal or cantonal server for the rectangle on screen and arrive as an image: this map cannot query them, date them from the picture, or check them. The headwaters are the exception, and are drawn in this map’s own ink.

    A headwater is not a spring. It is a reach with nothing above it, clipped at 5 km² of upstream area — the top of the network as this map draws it, and the part of the country with no gauge anywhere above it. The named springs and waterfalls that appear from zoom are swissNAMES3D’s 86 and 157 named ones, not a count of Switzerland’s springs.

    Only zones in force are drawn. geodienste.ch serves planned and future zones too; a planned zone is not yet a legal constraint. S1 is the fenced ground at the wellhead, S2 the close zone, S3 the outer one — Art. 20 GSchG, GSchV Annex 4 No. 1. They are measured in hectares, so at country view they are smaller than a pixel: zoom past a canton before expecting to see any.

    The water cycle, read as evidence

    Follow the water. Protect its pathways.

    The goal is to trace water through Switzerland—from ice and groundwater to rivers, storage, use, return and the border—and show the law protecting each essential path. The project exists to help protect wetlands and support legal action when protection fails. This release begins with surface water. Every figure states its source, age and evidence class.
